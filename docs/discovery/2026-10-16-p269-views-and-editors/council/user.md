### What Wokwi and Fritzing do, for each stage

- USER-14 · Wokwi's published diagram format has a breadboard-style editor, and its docs do not mention a schematic or a PCB view. The request says Wokwi does "also pcb and schema". I found no source that says it does. · E · https://docs.wokwi.com/diagram-format (read 2026-10-16, fetch result: "does not mention schematic or PCB views") · a feature search found only a feature-vote page (https://wokwi.com/features) and no schematic/PCB view · supports only: my search was two pages. That Wokwi has no such view is —. I did not open the editor (see part 2)
- USER-15 · Wokwi's format draws wires from a start pin to an end pin with a color, and optional straight "v" and "h" path hints. Wires never run diagonally. An empty color hides a wire. · E · https://docs.wokwi.com/diagram-format (read 2026-10-16) · supports only: Wokwi's wire model is a list of wires, not nets. Net highlight must be built by spark
- USER-16 · What Wokwi does well for the breadboard (from the project's own measurements): parts snap to a 9.6 px (2.54 mm) grid, a leg over a hole connects, the editor draws a pin overlay. A part turns about its center. · E · p267 README (measured 2026-10-16) · supports only: the breadboard grid is a good base for a perfboard grid (same pitch). Nothing about perfboard was measured
- USER-17 · What Wokwi does badly for spark's use: no read-back of his edits into spark, no step-by-step build mode, no per-step colors in its own UI (spark adds the colors), no stage switch, no catalogue. · E for the diagram format (no such field) · H for the UI (not opened) · supports only: these are gaps, not Wokwi faults
- USER-18 · Fritzing keeps three views of one project. A wire in the breadboard or schematic view shows in the other views as a dashed "ratsnest" line. Its users advise doing one view fully first. Forum threads show wrong ratsnest lines and "PCB connections completely incorrect". · E · https://www.electronicsforu.com/technology-trends/fritzing-a-practical-eda-tool-for-rapid-pcb-prototyping and https://forum.fritzing.org/t/ratsnest-lines-where-they-shouldnt-be-and-in-places-thats-clearly-connected/2652 (search snippets read 2026-10-16, pages not opened) · supports only: the three-view model is known to confuse. Each view holds its own geometry, so they can drift
- USER-19 · Fritzing cannot hold a breadboard and a stripboard of one circuit in one file: "keep it as two projects". Spark's J7 asks for exactly that, one circuit in four stages. · E · https://forum.fritzing.org/t/simultaneous-breadboard-and-stripboard-view/12869 (read 2026-10-16) · supports only: one forum answer, one user. J7 is a thing Fritzing does not do well

### UX patterns and the first slice

- USER-20 · Stage switch for one circuit: the stages share the nets and differ in geometry. A view of the same circuit must keep the same part names (R1, D3, LedRed5mm) as the sheet's steps. Today the sheet's names are the only link between the picture and the steps. · H (my design reading) · from jobs-and-journeys.md:41-44 and journey.md:420-432 · supports only: names are the cheapest cross-view link
- USER-21 · Net highlight across views: click a hole or a pin, light every pin on that net in all views. The data exists in the build (one built circuit writes PCB, Wokwi diagram and sheet). Wokwi's format cannot carry it, so it is a spark-side feature. · H · jobs-and-journeys.md:25 (J7 today: "each in its own way") for the data origin · supports only: it is a pattern that fits, not a proven one
- USER-22 · Build mode with step colors: the steps are numbered, each jumper has a step color, and the sheet already names the colors. A mode that shows step n, greys the rest, and ticks it off matches how he wires (one at a time, then tests). · E for steps and colors (journey.md:444-449) · H for the mode · supports only: the data is there already
- USER-23 · Edit and save back: his edit must survive the next build. Two shapes exist: an overlay of his moves kept beside the generated file, or a copy he owns that the build never overwrites. Which fits is — . A silent overwrite is the one outcome the guide already calls a loss. · E for the loss (journey.md:448-449) · H for the two shapes · supports only: two options named, none chosen
- USER-24 · Undo: any editor with drag needs it. Without it a wrong drop on a dense grid is costly, and his words "really nicely made ux" set the bar. · H · the request in brief.md:15 · supports only: a basic expectation, not a finding about him
