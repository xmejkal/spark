### PHYS — what the hardware does, in numbers

- **PHYS-1 · Module deep-sleep is 8 µA (RTC on) / 7 µA (RTC off), module only.** Provenance: datasheet-typ, 25 °C ("Typ" column), Table 12 p.15 of ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 datasheet v1.1, which I re-read from the kept copy (sha256 `bc430d66…`). Footnote 1 sits on the **Light-sleep** row ("—") and says to add 140 µA for 8 MB 8-line PSRAM (3.3 V), 200 µA (1.8 V), 40 µA for 2 MB 4-line; the Deep-sleep rows carry no footnote 1. The board record agrees (`power.deep_sleep_ua`: value null, "unmeasured"); the bin's CLAUDE.md says whether octal PSRAM draws in deep sleep "is a bench measurement (corrected 2026-10-01, spark P63)". · E `pdftotext -layout -f 14 -l 16` of the kept datasheet; `boards/firebeetle2-esp32s3.json` `power.deep_sleep_ua` · supports: 7–8 µA is the module's typical floor; it says nothing of the PSRAM in deep sleep, nor of the board around the module.
- **PHYS-2 · The board's own parts are not in that number and DFRobot publish none.** The record: "DFRobot publish no quiescent or deep-sleep figure for either SKU". The board adds a charger (ETA6003: "BATT to INPUT leakage current, Input Floating: 0 … 5 µA" — datasheet table, columns unlabelled in the text extraction, treated as an upper bound; I read the kept ETA6003 datasheet), a 3V3 buck (TPS62A02 on V1.2+, AXP313A PMIC on ≤V1.1; quiescent not read — H, model knowledge, not used below) and the USB-C/other parts. A DFRobot forum thread found by search reports 174 µA deep-sleep on a different FireBeetle 2 (DFR1139) "higher than the datasheet"; that is field data for another board, cited only as a reason the floor may be well above 8 µA. · E `boards/firebeetle2-esp32s3.json` `power`; kept ETA6003 datasheet; search result URL https://www.dfrobot.com/forum/topic/344118 (title "Firebeetle 2 High power consumption during deep sleep", read as a search-result summary, 2026-10-09, not opened) · supports: the board-level floor is unknown (—); a bench measurement is the only way to settle it.
- **PHYS-3 · Battery arithmetic for the node: the duty cycle, not the sleep current, decides life at any plausible period.** Formula (calculated): `I_avg = (I_sleep·(T−t_a) + I_a·t_a)/T`; life_days = `C·η / I_avg / 24`, with C = 1000 mAh (stated: drawer label "1S LiPo battery (1000 mAh)"), η = 0.8 usable (H, my assumption, no source). Active current I_a = 100 mA for t_a = 5 s is **H** (my guess for WiFi connect+send; no document read — the only datasheet figures are TX peaks, 355 mA @ 802.11b 1 Mbps and 286 mA HT20 MCS7, Table 11 p.15, datasheet-typ peak, "100% duty cycle"). Python run:

| I_sleep (provenance) | 100 mA × 5 s / 900 s | 100 mA × 5 s / 3600 s | 355 mA × 5 s / 900 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 µA (module, datasheet-typ) | 563 µA avg, 59 d | 147 µA, 227 d | 1980 µA, 17 d |
| 13 µA (+5 µA ETA6003 bound) | 569 µA, 59 d | 152 µA, 219 d | 1985 µA, 17 d |
| 148 µA (+140 if PSRAM draws; unmeasured) | 703 µA, 47 d | 287 µA, 116 d | 2119 µA, 16 d |
| 174 µA (field, other board) | 729 µA, 46 d | 313 µA, 107 d | 2145 µA, 16 d |

Sleep-only floors: 8 µA → 4167 d; 13 µA → 2564 d; 148 µA → 225 d; 174 µA → 192 d. · E calculated (python, this session); inputs as stated · supports: with a 15-minute WiFi report the PSRAM question moves the life from 59 to 47 days (20%), the WiFi on-time moves it much more; with a ≥1 h period the sleep floor starts to matter (227 → 116 d). It cannot show the real active energy: the 100 mA × 5 s is unmeasured.
- **PHYS-4 · USB device capability: S3 yes, C6 no.** ESP-IDF docs: "ESP32-C6 contains a USB Serial/JTAG Controller … a fixed-function USB device … cannot be reconfigured to perform any function other than a serial port and JTAG debugging functionality. This is in contrast to the USB OTG controllers in some ESP chips"; the ESP-IoT-Solution table lists ESP32-S3 USB OTG Full-Speed √ and ESP32-C6 USB OTG X, USB-Serial-JTAG √; FAQ: the S3's OTG interface "can not be used as USB Host and USB Device at the same time". Provenance: vendor documentation (stated), read 2026-10-09. The board: DFR0975 V1.3 schematic wires the USB-C D+/D- to `IO20`/`IO19` (module pins 14/13) — E schematic text; the board record's `pins` has no GPIO19/20 on the header and no usb field. · E the Espressif docs search (below) results: https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32c6/api-guides/usb-serial-jtag-console.html, https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-iot-solution/en/latest/usb/usb_overview/usb_overview.html (the support table), https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-faq/en/latest/software-framework/peripherals/usb.html · supports: a USB MIDI device (a custom descriptor) is S3-only among the library's boards; the XIAO C6 cannot present a MIDI device however it is wired. A C6 controller could only talk MIDI over BLE or a UART (not a USB MIDI foot controller as named).
- **PHYS-5 · On the S3, starting the USB device stack takes the USB-C away from the serial console.** Espressif: both controllers share one internal PHY; "once USB-OTG occupies the internal PHY, the USB Serial/JTAG will be disabled"; to flash again, "manually boot into download mode". Also an errata: S3 chips made before date code 2219 / PW-2022-06-XXXX have USB-OTG *download* mode unavailable (not the device function). · E the same search results (https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-iot-solution/en/latest/usb/usb_overview/usb_phy.html; esp-chip-errata USBOTG-4289) · supports: the controller round's bench loop changes (BOOT held at reset to re-flash; REPL over USB gone); whether the PO's DFR0975 is a post-2219 unit is unknown (—). MicroPython's USB-MIDI route on the S3 (`usb.device`) is **H** (my model knowledge); the bin's record cites MicroPython v1.29 verified on the C6, nothing on the S3's USB; unread.
- **PHYS-6 · Switch debounce for a foot controller.** `tactile-button` record: `debounce_ms_typical` 40, `verified: false`, source "the conventional figure for a tactile dome switch; not measured on any specific part". For a MIDI note-on 40 ms of debounce is added before the message; whether that is audible is a bench/perception question, H. A foot switch is mechanical and differently sized. · E `parts/tactile-button.json` `facts.debounce_ms_typical` · supports: the record is stated-unverified; it cannot settle latency.
- **PHYS-7 · Two power designs on one SKU matter to any I2C sensor.** V1.2 and later have no I2C pull-ups; V1.1 and earlier have 5.1 kΩ (PMIC). Which revision the PO has is B21 (#15) — open. · E `boards/firebeetle2-esp32s3.json` `hardware_revisions`; bin CLAUDE.md "One SKU, two power designs" · supports: a node with an I2C sensor needs the pull-up question settled from the chip beside the USB-C, or the sensor's own pull-ups (the VL6180X carrier has 10 kΩ, record `facts.carrier_has_level_shifters`), per sensor.
