### Risks and assumptions

- PO-17 · Is anyone blocked without each job now? J1: yes, the PO. The PO's stated goal is "breadboard including working firmware" and #144 found nothing says how to put it on the board. J2: no one is blocked; J1 with a full erase and write works, only slower. J3: not blocked, and only matters once a second board is in use. J4: not blocked. J5: not blocked, no Arduino user known. · E for J1 (#144 body, brief jobs); H for the rest · supports: ladder order, J1 first.
- PO-18 · The PO is the only user (n = 1) and the only board is one FireBeetle 2 ESP32-S3. No board is on a USB port today. All jobs except J1 rest on belief about makers who have not installed spark. · E · brief.md Start and reach; scrum/VISION.md "Who it is for" (primary persona is Hypothesis, no outsider has installed it) · supports: J3 and J5 serve a second board and a second platform that no person has asked for beyond the PO's words.
- PO-19 · J1 assumptions that must be true: (a) the 4 MB image boots on the 16 MB board (PO-12); (b) the S3's native USB shows the board on a port at all (the P100 discovery noted that starting a USB device stack disables the console and needs download mode: docs/discovery/2026-10-09-p100-real-use/brief.md C11); (c) esptool finds the port without the BOOT and RESET dance, or the hint in scripts/deploy.py:38 suffices; (d) the firmware's `ready` is visible after Ctrl-C and Ctrl-D (scripts/deploy.py:6, plan item 6 says "a bench question"); (e) the sim and the board see the same flash layout (the sim loaded the image at 0x0). · E for each condition's existence (pointers above); the outcomes are unknown · supports: the P215 bench tests all five at once and cheaply.
- PO-20 · Riskiest belief overall: that a full-flash image proven in Wokwi's ESP32-S3 model boots identically on the physical DFR0975 (PO-12, PO-19 a and e). The simulator takes a 4 MB image; the board has 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, and a bootloader on 0x0 that a different MicroPython port may expect with a partition table. Cheapest test: the P215 bench itself. Run `deploy.py --dry-run`, then the flash; pass line: the REPL shows `ready` and `os.statvfs('/')` shows a filesystem. Fail line: any other. (A read-only precursor with no firmware change: `esptool flash-id` and `read-mac` on the board, to see the real flash size before the erase.) · H for the belief (Claude); the test is a bench step · supports: put read-only identity first at the bench.
- PO-21 · Second riskiest: that J2 (updates without a full erase) can keep the "passed in the simulator" guarantee. If the board is updated by copying files with mpremote, nothing ties the copied file to a passed run. Cheapest test: a read, not a bench. Decide the gate (hash of firmware/ files in the run log, like the image sha256), then check whether `run_scenario.py` can log the files' hashes. · H · scripts/run_scenario.py:46-50, :187 (image sha256 only) · supports: PO-7.
- PO-22 · Third: that "the board is the one the project is for" (J3) can be decided by facts that software reads. Chip and flash size can be read (PO-9, PO-10); PSRAM, revision and pin wiring cannot. If spark compares only what it can read, it will say "matches" for a board with the wrong revision (power and I2C pull-up differences: boards/firebeetle2-esp32s3.json:300). A false "matches" is the harm, the same family as the repo's stated rule that a skipped is not a pass. · E for the revision difference; H for the failure mode · supports: J3 must report "unknown" for what it cannot read, with the same exit code semantics as `could-not-run`.
- PO-23 · A destructive default: `deploy.py` erases the whole flash, which deletes whatever the board held (the vendor's firmware, a previous project's files). #192's value line says "the person's yes before the erase" but the script as read asks nothing; it relies on the person choosing to run it. · E · #192 body "Value proven by"; scripts/deploy.py:177-186 (no prompt, no --yes) · supports: P213 (/spark:deploy) must carry the yes, or an agent (Claude) will erase without one.
- PO-24 · Port selection with several boards or none: without `--port`, esptool "tries each port" (scripts/deploy.py:166). With two boards, J3's check and the erase could hit the wrong one. · E · scripts/deploy.py:166 · supports: J3 and the erase need the port named first; a board listing step (`mpremote devs`, PO-4) is the reuse.
- PO-25 · Pre-mortem (Klein): six months after shipping, the epic failed. Most likely stories, each H (Claude, from the findings above):
  1. The first outside user's board was a different S3 or an S3 variant (N4, no PSRAM, 4 MB), the image or filesystem did not boot, and `deploy.py` reported success because esptool finished. No `ready` check was ever automatic. (PO-12, PO-19)
  2. The check for "right board" matched on chip only, said yes, and a revision difference or a pin wiring difference burnt a module or lit nothing. (PO-22)
  3. A full erase destroyed a board's earlier files, and an agent ran it without a yes. (PO-23)
  4. The REPL path (J4) grew into a second firmware surface: the agent wrote behaviour live on the board, outside the simulator and outside the pin-map check, and spark's "facts, never behaviour" rule was gone in practice. (PO-7, PO-15)
  5. Platform support (J5) took the effort, one platform got half-served, and the MicroPython chain regressed. (PO-15; AGENTS.md "pause tooling that does not serve this bar")
  6. Nobody used it: the PO is n = 1, and Thonny plus a copy of the files was enough for him. (PO-16, PO-18)
- PO-26 · What the P100 discovery already recorded about this area: "Firmware that runs" was not reachable from the persona's documents (★C6), and the bench step was "not yet". That is the root of P192. · E · docs/discovery/2026-10-09-p100-real-use/brief.md:53 · supports: J1 is the one job with prior discovery evidence.

## 2. What I could not check, and why

- Whether a 4 MB flash image at 0x0 boots on the 16 MB N16R8 and mounts a filesystem: needs the board (none on USB, brief Start and reach), and the MicroPython S3 partition table was not read from the build (one-search budget used elsewhere).
- Licences of mpremote/MicroPython (MIT), PlatformIO Core, Thonny, ViperIDE, esptool-js at source: not read; the search for PlatformIO's licence returned nothing usable. Marked H or — above.
- Whether esptool 5.5.0 has a PSRAM or eFuse-revision read usable for J3: the docs summary listed only read-mac, flash-id and read-flash in that section; `chip-id`, `get-security-info` and `read-efuse` were not checked. A fuller read of the esptool 5.x command reference is a one-fetch job.
- Thonny's and ViperIDE's actual capabilities, and whether either could replace esptool for a first flash: not read.
- Whether MicroPython v1.29.0 ESP32_GENERIC_S3 supports OTA partitions or an `ota` module out of the box: not read; the search found only third-party libraries.
- The PlatformIO and arduino-cli upload paths to a FireBeetle 2 ESP32-S3 (variant name, upload port behaviour): not read; arduino-esp32's variant header is cited by the board file but not opened.
- The Miro journey board and the P100 discovery's other council files were not read beyond one grep; scrum/WORKING_AGREEMENTS.md and docs/guide/agents.md were not read.
- The merged P192 deploy plan (docs/2026-10-12-p192-deploy-plan.md) was grepped, not read whole; the tests of `deploy.py` were not read.
- Nothing was run (no dry run), by the lens's read-only rule.
