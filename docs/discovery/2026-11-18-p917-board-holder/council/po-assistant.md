Risks and assumptions

- RISK-1 · A record binds only a user who reads it. The second user, pixel-games, goes through its own `tools/box.py` (mpremote) and runs spark plugin 0.4.0 without P892's gate. So the record cannot stop its touches. Its worth then is as a thing that the pixel-games session reads on its own will ("read through one spark command", scope line in the brief). · E (account) · brief C6, C7; `~/Development/pixel-games/tools/` is not on this Mac at that path (see "could not check"). · supports only: the hold is advisory for the one user who needs it. The record's value then equals the other session's habit of calling `spark` to read it, which is no different from reading the card today. Risk that the record is a second, weaker copy of the card comment.
- RISK-2 · Nobody is blocked now: the agreement of 15:43 works by messages and a card comment, and the PO is the only person and the two sessions' only decider. The one use that needs it, step 2c of P904 (#1283), is one hold of 60 to 90 minutes (C11). · E · card #1298 agreement; brief C11 (an estimate). · supports only: the need is one event for one pair of sessions. Not-blocked is true today. The value is in repeat use and in the PO's "might be good to leave in Spark in general" (his words, comment 1: H as a wish, no named second user beyond the pixel-games session). n = 1.
- RISK-3 · The riskiest belief: "a hold record will be read before a touch." It needs (a) the writer to write when it takes, (b) the reader to read before it touches, (c) the hold to end right. Each fails silently: a missed write looks the same as a free board. Cheapest test: do the take of step 2c with a hand-written hold file (or the card comment alone as today) and check, at the end, whether both sessions wrote and read it each time (a log of 5 deploys and 5 to 10 reads, C11), with no code. Pass line: —, for the decider (Q1). · H (mine) · pointer: brief C11, Q1 not answered. · supports only: a test before building.
- RISK-4 · A hold that ends by its clock is wrong in both directions: a session that stops early leaves the board marked held until the stated time (the agreement item 5, "the PO's bench work comes first", needs a manual release), and a session that overruns finds its hold expired while it still works (C11: 60 to 90 minutes with hard resets). Fencing is not possible here (EXIST-10). · E (pattern) + H (the overrun case) · EXIST-10, brief C11. · supports only: the end rule needs a renew and a release, not only an end time. The PO's bench interrupts break "ends by itself".
- RISK-5 · What "who" is, is undecided and every choice is lossy. A session has no stable id in the store (a restart loses it; the card comment says "a session that restarts reads the card"). A project is a folder name in the deploy record and a resolved path in the kept board (EXIST-4), and `walk3`/`bench` collide. Two sessions on one project, or one session touching two projects, break a project holder. · E · EXIST-1, EXIST-4; card "Open: who is a holder". · supports only: the holder key cannot be taken from the existing records as they stand.
- RISK-6 · Two stale records already exist in the store: the expired deploy grant from 2026-10-19 was never removed, and `projects.json` lists scratch folders. Hold files would join them unless something removes them. · E · the store's `grants/8c-bf-...-deploy.json`; `projects.json` entries under `/private/tmp`. · supports only: nothing cleans up an end-time record today, so "what a stopped session leaves behind" (card open question 3) is already the normal state of the store.
