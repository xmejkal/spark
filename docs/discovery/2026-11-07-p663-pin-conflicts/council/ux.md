## Questions for the decider

- Is a conflict that needs an answer one of the three outcomes today (`problems`, exit 1), or a fourth word, or a `could-not-run` with a `next` step that carries the choices? · decides: the exit code and the status word that every interface keys on (UX-5, UX-23).
- Should the assigner's `--json` print a JSON answer on a refusal (and on an open question) in the envelope shape of look and deploy (`status`, `problems` with a `code`, `next` steps), or a smaller shape of its own? · decides: whether Claude gets a conflict as data (UX-2, UX-3, UX-7).
- Where does an answer live: in `requirements.json` (the `signals` pin list or a new key), in the project's `.spark/` records, or in the board record? · decides: whether the next run keeps the answer and who can read it without the script (UX-9). Not recommended here.
- Is a kind that needs no choice (K3, K7, and K5 when it is a typo) told as a note or a warning on a run that stays `ok`, or does it stop the run? · decides: the amount of stops a person meets (UX-13, UX-15, UX-17).
- Does `/spark:idea` run the assigner so that the conflict is met when the part is picked, or only `/spark:build`? · decides: when the person is asked, and whether the question comes before the person has bought or wired anything (UX-20).
- Which of the sentence-first or data-first design is the contract: a command page that holds the question text (as the LED-level question does) or data that holds it (the question, the choices and the consequence in the `--json`, and the page only says "ask it")? · decides: whether a page repeats logic (UX-8, UX-21; AGENTS.md "an agent prompt never repeats its logic").

## Candidates only: what a good question looks like

These are candidates for the decider. They decide nothing.

- A question, in plain words, holds six things: (1) the one sentence that says what two things want the same pin (both claims named, with the part and the signal, and the pin by its printed pad and its GPIO number); (2) the choices, two to four, each one a short phrase; (3) which choice is the default, and why (what spark would do without an answer); (4) for each choice, the consequence in one sentence the person can check on the bench (for example "the pin map says GPIO3 for DC, and the sheet says plug the cable; GPIO3 is read at reset, so a display that pulls it low at reset can stop the board from booting"); (5) a code for the kind of question, so Claude keys on the code and not on the English; (6) the command that records each answer, with its dry run.
- One question at a time, as `commands/idea.md:46` does ("at most three questions, one at a time, each naming the need it could change"). When two kinds meet on one connector (the cable's five pins: K1, K2, K3 together), they are one question ("cable or wires?") and not five. D1 already chose that.
- Sketch, the cable question (D1) as data a page could turn into a question: kind `connector_choice`; subject `dfr0664-tft-2in` on `GDI`; choices `cable` (default, the 5 pins the connector fixes are placed first: GPIO21 BL, 18 CS, 3 DC, 38 RST, 9 SD_CS; the sheet says plug the ribbon cable) and `wires` (spark chooses free pins and the sheet lists five wires; a strapping pin stays refused); the effect of each on the pin map; answer written by a dry-run-then-write command. This is a candidate shape from D1's wording and `facts.gdi_on_firebeetle2_s3_gpio` in the part record (unverified: walk4 diary), not a settled design.
- Candidate for a kind that needs no choice (K3, backlight and the LED on GPIO21): one line, no stop: "TFT_BL and ONBOARD_LED share GPIO21: the LED blinks with the backlight." The person's only action is to continue; the data carries a `kind` and both holders.
- Candidate for a kind with no answer yet possible (K4, two connectors sharing pins, unverified image read): not a question; a note that the pins are unverified and which two connectors would be plugged at once. The question appears when the person says they use both.
- A bad question, seen today: "TFT_DC asks for D2 by name, and GPIO3 is strapping ... drop `pin` and spark picks another, or see P527 (#726)". It gives no choices, offers an action that breaks the wiring, and points at a card number.
