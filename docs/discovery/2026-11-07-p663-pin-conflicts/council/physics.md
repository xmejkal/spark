## 1. Findings

PH-1 · GPIO3 (DC on the GDI cable): the chip's own default for GPIO3 is "Floating", with no internal pull. At reset the latch samples it, but with the eFuses at their defaults (all 0) the datasheet's JTAG source table says the source is the USB Serial/JTAG controller and GPIO3 is "Ignored". So with a factory-fresh chip the level on DC cannot change the boot. It matters only if EFUSE_STRAP_JTAG_SEL is burned to 1 (then 1 = USB JTAG, 0 = pad JTAG pins MTDI/MTCK/MTMS/MTDO, which are GPIO41/39/42/40). · E · S3DS Table 3-1 (p.32, "GPIO3 Floating"), Table 3-5 (p.35, row EFUSE_STRAP_JTAG_SEL=0 → "Ignored"), s3 §3.4 text ("does not have any internal pull resistors and the strapping value must be controlled by the external circuit that cannot be in a high impedance state"), and "The default values of all the above eFuse parameters are 0" (p.32); WROOM Table 4/5 (p.12) says the same · supports: K2 is a bookkeeping conflict on a default chip and a real one only on a chip with the eFuse burned. Whether the PO's chip has it burned is — (not read; see part 2).

PH-2 · What level DC holds at reset: on the screen module DC goes through the 6-channel buffer U3 (input 4A, then R6 390R to the LCD). I read no pull-up or pull-down on the DC line in the module schematic; R4 10K to VCC sits on the RES line, R11 100K to VCC on CS, R10 100K to 3 V on SDCS. On the FireBeetle, IO3 goes to the header and to GDI P2 pin 12 only; I read no resistor on it. So both ends are high impedance and the level at reset is not set by anything: it floats. The record `DFR0664.facts.on_board_pulls` lists RES, CS, SDCS and BL and no DC, which agrees. · E (read off images) · LCD page 1 (P2 pin 6 DC → U3 4A; R4, R11, R10 positions), FB13 page 1 (P2 pin 12 = IO3; no resistor on that net), `DFR0664` facts.on_board_pulls · supports: the fact "DC has no pull" is a record fact a check can read. U3's part type is not named in LCD; its input leakage is not known (`DFR0664.facts.input_high_v` is null), so how a floating input settles is H (CMOS inputs drift; not measured).

PH-3 · The boot-mode strap that does matter on this board is GPIO0 (D9): default weak pull-up (1). GPIO0 = 0 with GPIO46 = 0 (GPIO46 default weak pull-down) is download boot; GPIO0 = 0 with GPIO46 = 1 is "invalid and will trigger unexpected behavior". The FireBeetle ties GPIO0 to the BOOT button through R9 470R (button to ground), and GDI P2 pins 1 and 2 are labelled GPIO1 and GPIO0 but carry no net. So a part on D9 that idles low at power-up stops the boot into download mode. · E · S3DS Table 3-1, Table 3-3 (p.33), WROOM Table 5 note 1 (p.12), FB13 page 1 (R9 470R/1%, K3) · supports: a strap is a damage-free but boot-stopping conflict, and the fact needed is the strap's default level plus which level a part holds at reset. The record's strapping note for GPIO0 is right. Its note for GPIO3 ("neither is safe for something that idles low") is stronger than the datasheet on a default chip (see PH-1).

PH-4 · GPIO21 (BLK and the onboard LED): on the board, IO21 → R15 2K/1% → LED1 → GND. On the screen, BLK → R3 1K → base of NPN Q1 (emitter GND), with R2 10K from VCC to the BLK node. Q1's collector runs through R1 10R (as drawn) to the backlight LED cathode (LEDK); LEDA is on 3 V. So one pin drives a 2K LED branch and a transistor base in parallel. Calculated source current from IO21 when high (inputs: VDD 3.3 V stated as the S3's I/O rail, Vbe 0.7 V assumed H typical for a small NPN, R3 1000 ohm and R15 2000 ohm from the schematics, LED Vf not known): base branch (3.3−0.7)/1000 = 2.6 mA; LED branch (3.3−Vf)/2000 = 0.75 / 0.65 / 0.55 / 0.15 mA for Vf 1.8 / 2.0 / 2.2 / 3.0 V; total about 2.8 to 3.4 mA. At VOH = 2.64 V (the datasheet's own test point) the base branch is 1.94 mA. Against IOH = 40 mA (datasheet-typ, VDD 3.3 V, VOH ≥ 2.64 V, PAD_DRIVER = 3) that is about 7 to 9 percent. When the pin is low it sinks R2's 0.33 mA (3.3/10000, only if VCC on the module is 3.3 V) which is 1 percent of IOL = 28 mA. No overload either way. · calculated, with E for resistor values · FB13 page 1 (R15 2K/1%, LED1 on IO21), LCD page 1 (R2 10K, R3 1K, Q1, R1 10R), S3DS Table 5-4 (p.65) · supports: K3 is bookkeeping, not damage. The record needs the fact "BLK and the onboard LED are one net (GPIO21)", which `DFR0664.facts.gdi_backlight_gpio_on_firebeetle2_s3` already holds (verified: true, bench 2026-11-01).

PH-5 · What the person sees on GPIO21: high lights the LED and turns the backlight on; low turns both off; PWM at a duty dims both together; the LED cannot be used as a status light independent of the screen. The PO saw exactly this ("backlight is kind of glowing like the LED, following the same rhythm", walk 4 diary, 19:52 CET). Before firmware runs (GPIO21 not driven) R2 pulls the BLK node to VCC through 10K+1K, so the base gets (3.3−0.7)/11000 = 0.24 mA: calculated, and by the module record's own note ("BL open means the backlight is on"; `DFR0664.facts.on_board_pulls`, verified: false) the screen is lit at boot. The collector current is set by R1 10R and the LED string, not by the base, so 0.24 mA of base may or may not saturate Q1; whether the backlight is full or dim at boot is — (needs the transistor and the LED forward voltage). · E (walk 4 diary 19:52 row) for the follow, calculated for the boot figure · supports: nothing beyond the K3 text; the question of removing "the LED" has no hardware answer, since the LED is soldered to the net.

PH-6 · The GDI and camera connectors share IO1 and IO2, and these are the board's I2C pair (SDA IO1, SCL IO2, header labels SDA and SCL). I read the same in the diary: GDI P2 pin 5 SDA = IO1, pin 6 SCL = IO2, camera P1 pin 3 SDA = IO1, pin 5 SCL = IO2. A shared I2C bus is not a conflict: it is open-drain by design, many devices on one pair. It becomes one only if (a) two devices answer at the same address (not recorded in any record I read), (b) there is no pull-up (V1.2 and later have none anywhere on the board; V1.1 and earlier have 5.1k from the PMIC) or (c) a part treats the pins as GPIO outputs. The DFR0664 uses none of GDI pins 11 to 18 (FCS, TCS, SCL, SDA, INT, BUSY/TE, GPIO1, GPIO2 have no net on the module), so with this screen nothing sits on the I2C pair. · E (BOARD.hardware_revisions; `DFR0664.facts.gdi_used_signals`; `~/Development/spark-walks/walk4/walk4-diary.md:81-83`) · supports: K4 is a shared bus for IO1/IO2 and needs the fact "this pin pair is a bus, with the pull-up status per revision", not a claim.

PH-7 · Not every shared pin of K4 is a bus. IO7 is GDI FCS (pin 8) and camera Y6 (pin 18); a camera data line is a driven output, FCS is an output of the host toward a font chip. Per the diary the pair is also the header pin D5. The camera connector also lands on IO4, IO5, IO6, IO8 (camera Y5, PCLK, VSYNC, Y7), which are header pins A0, A1, A2, A3 (BOARD.pins), the ADC1 pins a joystick wants. If a camera is plugged in at the same time as a joystick on A0 to A3, the net has two sources (an analog wiper and a driven camera line). This is a hardware revision read from a schematic image: the camera pin numbers come from walk 4's research agent, "verified: false". · E (diary:81-83 + BOARD.pins) · supports: the camera connector must be a record of its own (P460 #628), otherwise A0..A3 appear free while the connector holds them.

PH-8 · Which of the chip's restricted pins does the board record mark? Chip list read from S3DS §2.3.4 and §2.3.5 (p.25 to 26): strapping GPIO0, 3, 45, 46; USB Serial/JTAG GPIO19, 20; JTAG GPIO39 to 42; UART0 GPIO43, 44; octal flash/PSRAM GPIO33 to 37 (the higher four data bits and DQS) and GPIO26 to 32 (in-package flash/PSRAM). On the WROOM-1 module with octal PSRAM (N16R8, the DFR0975), "IO35, IO36, and IO37 connect to the OSPI PSRAM and are not available for other uses" (WROOM, §3.2 table note b, p.10). The record BOARD.pin_roles marks: strapping [0, 3] (no 45, 46), adc2_unusable_with_wifi [11..20] (includes 19, 20, but as ADC2, not as USB), console_uart [43, 44], onboard_led [21], onboard_button [47], not_wake_capable [38, 43, 44, 47], spi, i2c. It marks nothing for USB D-/D+ (GPIO19/20, on header P3 pins 3 and 2 as USB_D_N/USB_D_P in FB13), JTAG (39 to 42), or the octal PSRAM pins (35 to 37). Of the unmarked pins, the header exposes only 19 and 20 (labelled D- and D+, in `physical.header_order`, but absent from `pins`); 35 to 37 and 39 to 42, 45, 46, 48 reach the outside only through the camera connector, per diary:83. · E · S3DS pp.25 to 26, 32; WROOM p.10; BOARD.pin_roles; FB13 page 1 · supports: a pin-restriction table is a fact set a record can carry (chip restrictions as chip data, once, not per board); what the board exposes decides which restrictions can bite. 45, 46 and 48 are not in the board record at all, so a connector record that fixes camera pins on IO45 or IO46 has nothing to be checked against.

PH-9 · A strapping pin used by a connector: the camera connector puts XMCLK on IO45 and Y8 on IO46 (diary:83). GPIO45 (default pull-down, 0) selects the VDD_SPI voltage (0 = 3.3 V, 1 = 1.8 V); GPIO46 (default pull-down) is in the boot-mode pair. A camera that holds IO45 high at reset selects 1.8 V flash supply, which a 3.3 V flash on the module does not survive; whether the DFR0975 module's flash is on VDD_SPI at 3.3 V and whether the EFUSE overrides the strap is — (not read). The camera's reset-time output state on XMCLK and Y8 is not in any record. · E for the strap function (S3DS Table 3-1, Table 3-2 text on p.32), H for the damage claim (a general 1.8 V/3.3 V flash mismatch; I did not read the module's VDD_SPI wiring) · supports: a strap on a connector is the one case where a conflict can stop a boot, depending on what the plugged part holds at reset. That is a fact about the part (its reset-time level), not about the pin.

PH-10 · Two real output drivers on one net. Every K-case on this project is one GPIO pin with one driver, because the ESP has a single pin per net: K1 (GPIO18 given to I2S_BCLK while the cable's LCS is on IO18) puts one driver (the ESP's pin) on a net with two receivers, not two drivers. The receivers see signals they did not expect: the display CS input sees a 1.41 MHz clock (calculated: 44100 Hz x 32 bit slots; the BCLK rate depends on the firmware's sample rate and frame size, not read), and the amplifier BCLK input sees CS pulses. That scrambles audio and selects the display at random. No part is damaged. · calculated, H for the receiver behaviours (not run) · supports: K1 and K5 are functional conflicts (wrong behaviour, nothing burns).

PH-11 · The damaging case needs two drivers. Candidates here: a camera data output (Y5..Y9 on IO4, IO5, IO6, IO7, IO8, IO48) with the same pin also driven by the ESP as an output; a module output pin wired to a pin another module's output also drives (e.g. a font chip or SD card MISO, which the SD card drives toward GDI MISO, against a second device's output on the same net). If both drive opposite levels, current is limited only by the two output resistances. Estimate (formula and inputs): ESP output resistance R = (3.3 V − 2.64 V) / 40 mA = 16.5 ohm, from IOH's test point, which is datasheet-typ (the datasheet gives no output resistance); two such drivers against each other: 3.3 V / (2 x 16.5 ohm) = 100 mA calculated, an order of magnitude over the 40 mA test figure. The datasheet Table 5-1 as I read it lists no per-pin absolute maximum, only a cumulative 1500 mA, so I cannot say whether 100 mA through one pin is past a limit; that figure is — (the Technical Reference Manual is not in the store). · calculated, S3DS Table 5-4 (p.65), Table 5-1 (p.64) · supports: damage conflicts are rare and need a fact the records do not hold today: the direction of each pin of each part at reset (an output or a Hi-Z). The part records hold `direction` per need (`out`/`in`, from the ESP's side), but not the level or Hi-Z at reset.

PH-12 · ADC2 and WiFi (K7): GPIO11 to 20 are ADC2, unusable with WiFi on. The record lists them in `pin_roles.adc2_unusable_with_wifi` and keeps `adc_gpio` to GPIO1 to 10. On this board the GDI cable fixes SCLK on IO17, MOSI on IO15, MISO on IO16, CS on IO18, which are all ADC2 pins: irrelevant while they carry SPI (digital use is allowed with WiFi), but the pins that are left for analog input are IO1..IO10 minus those the cable and the camera take (IO1, IO2 for I2C; IO7, IO9, IO3 taken by the cable). The record states "usable" only as digital I/O. · E (BOARD.adc_gpio, pin_roles; diary:81) · supports: K7 is a cost, as the jobs file says; the fact needed is "this GPIO is ADC1 or ADC2", already held.

PH-13 · Two documents disagree on a fact that decides a conflict: the MAX98357A module's SD bias resistor (DFRobot's schematic: R1 = 100k from VCC to SD; DFRobot's wiki text: 680k, "mix" mode). The record keeps both with `verified: false`. Consequence for the pin conflict model: holding SD low (AUDIO_SHUTDOWN driven) costs 33 uA through 100k or 4.9 uA through 680k at 3.3 V (both calculated, per the record), neither a pin overload. Which channel the amp plays when SD is left floating also differs by revision (left vs mix). · E · `~/Development/spark-p663/parts/max98357a-dfr0954.json` facts.sd_bias_resistor_ohms · supports: not a pin conflict, but shows that a conflict's "who holds the pin by default" can rest on a verified: false fact.

PH-14 · Two hardware revisions disagree on facts that change conflict outcomes, quoted from the board record with pointers: V1.1 and earlier: "5.1k to 3V3 already present on SDA/SCL — they are the PMIC's own bus pull-ups, and the PMIC is another device on your I2C bus"; V1.2 and later: "NONE anywhere on the board. Every I2C device must bring its own". And for the button on GPIO47: V1.1 and earlier "reads ~0.7 V pressed, not 0 V", V1.2 and later "a plain button" (BOARD.hardware_revisions, citing FB13 and the V1.0 schematic). For GPIO3, GPIO21, GPIO0 I read V1.3 only; I did not read the V1.0 schematic (`107c5437...`) for IO3 or IO21, so whether R15 2K and the strap net are the same on V1.0 is — · E for the quotes · supports: a conflict check on I2C must read the revision, since "shared bus" has a different meaning with or without the PMIC and without the pull-ups.

PH-15 · The classes of conflict, by consequence, from what I read: (a) bookkeeping only: K1 where it moves a pin (wrong pins in pins.py vs the cable), K3 (one net, two jobs, within current limits), K4 on IO1/IO2 (a shared bus), K5 on distinct digital uses of different times; (b) boot-affecting when a level is held at reset: K2 (only with the JTAG eFuse burned), GPIO0 held low, GPIO45/46 by a connector part, a low-idling part on D9; (c) functional damage to the data, not hardware: two signals on one pin (PH-10); (d) hardware damage: two drivers (PH-11), and 5 V into a 3.3 V pin (out of scope here; check_physics owns electrical budgets per the brief). · calculated and E as cited above · supports: the model needs a severity per kind, and only (b) and (d) need facts about a level at reset.

PH-16 · What spark must know as a fact, never as behaviour, to name each case (each one a record fact with its source): the chip's restricted-pin table (strap, USB, JTAG, UART0, flash/PSRAM) per chip variant; for a strap pin its default level and what each level selects (a datasheet fact, with the eFuse that overrides it); per connector pin: which GPIO, which signal, whether it is wired on this part's module (the DFR0664 uses GDI 1 to 10 only); per board pin: its onboard load (the LED with its resistor, the button, the pull-ups) and whether it is on a bus; per part pin: its direction and its state at reset (driven, Hi-Z, floating, pulled with a resistor), which no record holds today (PH-2, PH-11); per I2C part: its address and whether it carries pull-ups. Spark does not need thresholds, timings or a driver. · H (my reading of the records against the cases above) · supports: records grow by need; this is a list of needs, in the order in which a conflict check would use them.

## 2. What you could not check, and why

- Whether the PO's chip has EFUSE_STRAP_JTAG_SEL, EFUSE_DIS_USB_JTAG or EFUSE_DIS_PAD_JTAG burned. Cheapest rung: `espefuse.py summary` over USB on the board, which reads and changes nothing. It cannot show the level that DC floats to.
- The level on DC at reset (PH-2). Cheapest rung: a multimeter or scope on GDI P2 pin 12 (IO3) at power-up with the screen attached. It would not show what a different screen copy or revision of the buffer does. No simulation settles it: Wokwi has no ST7789V part (`DFR0664.simulation.skip`), and a Wokwi limit from the S3 devkit stand-in is assumed, not read, for this board; I read no dated Wokwi feature table.
- The backlight current at full brightness, LED forward voltage and the transistor's collector current (PH-4, PH-5). `DFR0664.facts.peak_current_ma` is null. Cheapest rung: a meter in series with LEDA at the 3 V supply of the module. It would not show a different panel batch.
- Whether Q1 saturates on the 0.24 mA of base current available at boot through R2 and R3 (PH-5): needs the transistor's type (not named in LCD) and the LED string. A bench look at the screen with GPIO21 not yet driven is the cheapest rung.
- The V1.0 schematic for IO3, IO21, IO0 (PH-14); I read V1.3 only. The camera connector pins are from walk 4's reading of the V1.3 schematic image, not read again by me, and the diary marks them unverified.
- The module's VDD_SPI wiring and whether the flash on the N16R8 is 3.3 V (PH-9), the ESP32-S3 Technical Reference Manual's per-pin limits (PH-11), and the camera module's reset-time output state. None of them is in the store.
- The MAX98357A's own datasheet for GAIN/SD behaviour (copied from the record, not re-read), the joystick's datasheet (none; the record is from photos). I did not read the Arduino variant header or soc_caps.h that the board record cites.
- DFR0664 U3's part type: not printed on the module schematic.
- The Espressif docs MCP tool: not used (the kept datasheets answered the strapping questions).

## 3. Questions for the decider

- Is a severity per conflict kind part of the first slice (a stop for boot-affecting and contention kinds, a note for bookkeeping)? Decides: whether the model carries a severity field, or only the kind and the two claims.
- Does the model hold a part pin's state at reset (driven low, driven high, Hi-Z, floating, pulled by R to rail) as a record fact, and which parts get it first (the screen, the camera, the amplifier)? Decides: whether PH-10 and PH-11 can be told apart by data, or only by a person.
- Should the chip's restricted-pin table (strap, USB, JTAG, flash/PSRAM) be added to the board record or live with the chip? Decides: where the 45, 46, 19, 20 and 35 to 37 marks go, and whether a camera connector with pins that the header does not have can be checked.
- Is `espefuse.py summary` (read-only) allowed as the one bench step for the JTAG eFuse? Decides: whether K2 on the PO's board is closed as bookkeeping or stays open.
- Is the camera connector in or out of the first slice? Decides: whether IO4 to IO8, IO45, IO46 and IO48 enter the model at all (PH-7, PH-9).
