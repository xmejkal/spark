# P235: the reach test (A1) on the XIAO ESP32-C6, 2026-10-15

The riskiest belief of P235's discovery is A1: mpremote reaches a running C6 outside a deploy. The PO chose to test it
at once (D4). The pass line, written before the run: 19 of 20 answers. Result: 17 of 20, missed.

## Setup

- Board: Seeed XIAO ESP32-C6, MAC 8C:BF:EA:CC:97:70, port /dev/cu.usbmodem1101, on the Mac's own USB port.
- Firmware: button-light (`light.py`, `main.py`, `pins.py`, `spark.json`). It reads its console without waiting.
- Each try: `timeout 20 mpremote connect /dev/cu.usbmodem1101 exec "print(1)"`. A pass is the answer `1` and exit 0.
- mpremote 1.29.0 on Python 3.10.0.

## Half 1: the firmware running, 10 of 10

A first run of 10 tries, 5 s apart (08:17:41 to 08:18:29), gave 10 of 10. It does not count. After the first exec the
firmware no longer ran, so tries 2 to 10 met an idle board.

The counted run (08:19:07 to 08:20:42): `mpremote reset`, then 10 s, then the try, 10 times. All 10 answered `1`.
A check after the same steps, with `resume` so that no soft reset ran, listed `['flashbdev', 'light', 'machine',
'pins']`. So the firmware ran when the try reached it, and its Ctrl-C got through.

## Half 2: after a replug, 7 of 10

The PO unplugged and replugged the cable 10 times. A watcher waited 10 s after the port came back, then tried.

All three misses said `mpremote.transport.TransportError: could not enter raw repl`.

## After the first miss

- 08:25:09: a second try, with `resume`, failed the same way.
- Then a port open with DTR and RTS off, to listen. The board restarted: `rst:0x15 (USB_UART_HPSYS),boot:0x1e
  (SPI_FAST_FLASH_BOOT)`, then the ROM lines and `ready`.
- 08:25:32: the try with `resume` answered `['flashbdev', 'light', 'machine', 'pins']`.

So after some power-on boots, the firmware runs but mpremote cannot stop it, until a USB-side reset. The cause is not
proven. Hypotheses: (H1) a print blocks while the USB host is not yet reading, so Ctrl-C waits; (H2) the USB-Serial-JTAG
link comes up in a state where the Ctrl-C bytes do not reach the firmware's stdin; (H3) 10 s after plug-in is too early
on some boots. None was tested. 3 of 10 replugs is one sample on one board and one Mac.

## Total

17 of 20 (10 of 10 after a reset, 7 of 10 after a replug). The pass line was 19 of 20. Missed: by D4's line, the reach
after a power-on is fixed before a live command is built.

## The cause hunt (round 3 of P238's spike, D5), 08:32 to 08:49

A probe opened the port as mpremote does on macOS (pyserial's default open, `dtr True rts True`), listened 2 s, sent
`\r\x03\r\x01` (Ctrl-C, Ctrl-A) and listened 3 s more. A watcher ran it 10 s after each replug.

- Round A, 10 replugs: 9 reached the raw REPL (the Ctrl-C stopped `light.py` at line 51). Replug 7 was silent: no
  byte on open, no byte after Ctrl-C and Ctrl-A.
- Round B, 10 replugs, with `esptool --before no-reset --after no-reset chip-id` on a silent board: 6 of 10 silent.
  esptool failed each time with `Invalid head of packet (0x08): Possible serial noise or corruption.` The chip
  sent bytes, but not the ROM's answer, so it was not in download mode.
- Round C, replug until silent (replug 4 of 4): the probe sent code that toggles GPIO15, the on-board LED, 6 times.
  No byte came back. The PO saw the LED blink 3 times. So the input reached the firmware and the code ran. Only the
  output to the Mac was lost.
- On that silent board: two more `mpremote ... resume exec` tries (08:47:46, 08:47:58) failed with `could not enter
  raw repl`. A soft reset (Ctrl-B, Ctrl-D) did not bring the output back (08:48:18). A port open with DTR and RTS
  off restarted the chip (`rst:0x15 (USB_UART_HPSYS)`), it printed `ready`, and `print(1)` answered at 08:48:39.
  The same cure worked at 08:25.

What is proven, on one board, one Mac and one firmware: after some power-on boots, the C6's USB output to the host
stays dead while its input works, until a chip reset from the USB side. A soft reset does not cure it. How often:
1, 3, 6 and 1 of 10, 10, 10 and 4 replugs. The rate varies, and nothing here explains why.

Not proven: the cause inside MicroPython or the chip. A rival is not ruled out: the bytes can be lost on the Mac's side (its USB driver or pyserial), because the cure also makes the Mac enumerate the port again. A lead, not a match: a MicroPython report of "no feedback after
power on" on the USB terminal, input accepted, fixed by `CONFIG_ESP_CONSOLE_USB_SERIAL_JTAG=y`
(https://notes.alelec.net/assets/mpy-triage/pair/3132.html, read 2026-10-15). That report's output never worked,
ours fails on some boots only.
