# Walk 1: the reaction game on the XIAO ESP32-C6 (P326, 2026-10-19)

Card: https://github.com/xmejkal/spark/issues/418. Board: XIAO ESP32-C6. Parts at hand: jumpers, buttons, no
resistors. Project folder: `~/Development/spark-walks/reaction-game`.

## Predictions (written 2026-10-19 evening, Prague, before the walk)

Written by Claude before any step ran. Someone who did not write them scores them after the walk.

1. `/spark:init` writes the project files and asks for the board. It works with no stop.
2. `/spark:idea` turns the goal into needs: a button, a light and the console. It will not pick the board's own LED
   unless the person says so, and it offers an external LED with a resistor.
3. `/spark:build` builds a board with one external button. It cannot mix one external part with the board's own LED
   in one requirements file, so this is the first stop.
4. The breadboard sheet asks for a resistor that the PO does not have, or it does not say that the internal pull-up
   is used (P204).
5. Claude writes the firmware, and `import_firmware.py` passes on the Mac.
6. The Wokwi scenario passes for the button and the console lines. Its record does not say that the LED and the
   timing were not covered (P345 is not built).
7. `look.py` matches the board record, and `deploy.py` puts the image on the board with `ready seen`. One retry or
   one press of BOOT or RESET is needed.
8. The change by words and the second deploy pass with no new stop.
9. The whole walk takes 60 to 90 minutes and two or more stops that need a card.

## The walk

Times are Prague time, 2026-10-19 evening.

1. Init: `init_project.py --board xiao-esp32-c6` wrote every file with no stop. It left `goal`, `prefer` and `sellers`
   null, as designed. Claude filled the goal, five musts and the PO's Czech shops from the card. `check_all.py` listed
   two board definitions (the FireBeetle too) for a project that uses one.
2. Idea: three needs (board, button, light). `--match` offered no "board's own LED" for the light need, only LED
   matrices and a 5 mm LED. The light need stayed without a pick.
3. Idea, the pick: refused, because the drawer held the one XIAO for plant-alarm. The PO said yes, and
   `--drawer-set` moved the hold to this project. `requirements.json` names the XIAO and one button, with light under
   `unserved`. Tally: 2 picks, both owned, 0 requests, 2 min.
4. Build: the chain ran end to end with no stop. The verdict says "not every need is on the board: light (no pick)",
   though the board's LED serves it. `pins.py` names `ONBOARD_LED = 15` and says its polarity is not in the board
   record. The sheet has two jumpers and says that the internal pull-up replaces the resistor. The simulation line
   warns that no serial monitor is wired for the XIAO's part.
5. Firmware: Claude wrote `game.py` (one adapter class for the button and the LED, one round function, and LED_ON
   as the one unknown level). `import_firmware.py` passed.
6. Simulation: not run. The PO decided that Wokwi runs only rarely, because the CI minutes cost (his words on P337
   #431). `deploy.py` flashes only an image that passed a run, so spark had no path to the board.
7. Bench, look: `look.py` matched the board record (chip esp32c6, 4 MB flash, MAC 8c:bf:ea:cc:97:70) and gave no
   token, because no image had passed a run.
8. Bench, the copy: with the PO's yes, Claude copied `firmware/` with `mpremote`, outside spark. The PO had no
   breadboard, so the game moved to the XIAO's own B button, as GPIO9 typed by hand (the record has no GPIO for it).
9. The change by words: the PO asked for a surprise LED effect at each press, and a speed that changes while the
   button is held. Claude wrote `effects.py` (seven effects, PWM dimming), and `main.py` starts it. spark's import
   check stopped, because its stand-in `machine` has no `PWM`. After the copy and a reset, the PO said:
   *"great, works like a charm"*. That confirms LED_ON = 0 and GPIO9 for B on this board.

## Against the card

Done: init, idea, build, firmware, the sheet, the look, a first copy and a change by words with a second copy, on the
PO's XIAO. Not done: the Wokwi scenario, `deploy.py`, a wired button, and the reaction game itself as seen by the PO
(he played it, but the console output did not reach the record). The two copies went around spark.
