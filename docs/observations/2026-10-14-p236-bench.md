# P236 bench: the board-only blink on the XIAO ESP32-C6

Run 2026-10-14 by the PO and Claude, card [P236 (#233)](https://github.com/xmejkal/spark/issues/233). Project
`~/Development/blink-c6`, board MAC 8c:bf:ea:xx:xx:xx on `/dev/cu.usbmodem101` (the Mac's own port).

## Short version

The blink project ran end to end. It was built board-only (PR #247), simulated in Wokwi (passed), flashed through
`deploy.py` with the PO's yes, and checked: ready seen, three files match, facts file written and read back. The LED
blinks, and typed lines change the period. One step failed: right after the flash, esptool's reset left the chip in
its ROM download mode, so mpremote heard nothing and ready was unknown. A press of the R button started the firmware.

## What happened, in order

1. The board did not answer at first: esptool said "Serial data stream stopped: Possible serial noise or corruption",
   and mpremote said "could not enter raw repl". The PO pressed R and B a few times. A port watch logged eleven
   drops and returns, so both buttons work. They are the small parts at the two top corners, marked R and B.
2. `esptool flash-id`: 4 MB. `look.py`: chip same, flash same, token `ae7b-debd`.
3. The PO ran `deploy.py --token ae7b-debd` in his own terminal and typed yes. The flash ran. Then:
   `facts file: not written: ... could not enter raw repl` and `ready unknown: ... could not enter raw repl`.
4. Claude opened the port with DTR and RTS kept off and read `waiting for download`: the ROM boot mode.
5. Again, with no mpremote: `esptool chip-id` (which ends "Hard resetting via RTS pin..."), then the same listen.
   The chip printed `waiting for download` again. So esptool's reset alone leaves this chip in download mode.
6. The PO pressed R once. The listen heard the ROM boot lines, then `ready`. Claude sent `200`, and the firmware
   answered `period 200 ms`.
7. mpremote then worked: MicroPython 1.29.0, `SEEED_XIAO_ESP32C6`, files `blink.py`, `main.py`, `pins.py`.
8. The ReadyCheck and FactsFile classes, called by hand as `deploy.py` calls them: "ready seen | the firmware printed
   'ready' and the board's 3 files match the run that passed". The facts file was written and read back. The store
   record was updated.

9. Two more deploys of changed firmware (echo at 14:03, Backspace at 14:12) said "ready seen" by themselves. At
   14:12 the PO did not press R before `deploy.py` finished. So esptool's reset after the write booted the firmware.

## The cause, as far as it is proven

After esptool's hard reset over the chip's USB-Serial-JTAG, this XIAO ESP32-C6 boots into the ROM download mode, not
from flash (steps 4 and 5). A press of RESET boots from flash (step 6). This explains the silent console of the P215
bench (AT131), where the hypothesis was USB re-enumeration. But it does not happen every time (step 9). Each time it
happened, the chip was already in download mode before esptool ran: the PO had pressed BOOT and R at 13:43, and at
step 5 the chip still waited from step 4. Each time it worked, the firmware ran before the flash.

Hypothesis, not tested: a chip that waits in download mode goes back to it after esptool's reset, and a chip that ran
its firmware boots from flash. The test for P238 (#235): flash from each state, three times, and log what the console
prints first.
